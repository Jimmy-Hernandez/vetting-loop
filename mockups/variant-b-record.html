After: The Accountability Trail

What the public record actually says about 7 August 2024 — the day the National Assembly approved 19 Cabinet Secretaries. Every line on this page is traceable to a filed document; where the record is silent, we say so.

Record of proceedings· 1 Hansard sitting· 1 committee report· 4 filed sources· Non-partisan

Four words carry the whole decision. The House approved nineteen Cabinet Secretaries by voice vote. No division was called — which means no Member’s name was ever recorded on any of the nineteen approvals. This register reproduces what does exist, and marks plainly what does not.

Rejected — clause (ii)

Ms. Stella Soi Lang’at

Nominee — Cabinet Secretary, Ministry of Gender, Culture, the Arts & Heritage. The only nominee the House declined, on the Committee’s recommendation.

The Committee found that she “did not demonstrate adequate understanding of the docket to which she had been nominated” and noted that her public-service experience was “marked by very frequent job transitions in various roles” across six postings.2 Moving the motion, the Majority Leader added that the rejection was “in no way an indictment of the capability or competence of a nominee to perform other responsibilities”.1

The EACC “confiscated my children’s phones” at the Integrity Centre — yet “I have never been called to record a statement.”

— Wycliffe Oparanya, as reported in his own account of the EACC investigation, Daily Nation, 4 August 20243

  1. 29 Jul 2024

    EACC writes to the Clerk. In response to the Assembly’s background-check request, the anti-graft commission states that the nominee’s integrity is in question: it is investigating alleged corruption, abuse of office, money laundering and conflict of interest from his time as Kakamega County Governor, and says it has secured court orders freezing assets amounting to ~Sh28.9 million.3

  2. 4 Aug 2024

    He answers at the hearing. Before the Committee on Appointments, Oparanya denies the allegations, says the EACC “confiscated my children’s phones” during its raid and that he went to the Integrity Centre only to retrieve them — and states “I have never been called to record a statement” on the allegations.3 The Committee’s record of its own questioning is contained in its Second Report.2

  3. 7 Aug 2024

    The Committee reports to the House. Committee member Hon. Junet Mohamed (Suna East, ODM) tells the House: allegations of corruption were raised against the nominee — “We clarified that the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) and the Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP) wrote to Parliament and said there were no charges against him. Based on that, we made a decision.”1

  4. 7 Aug 2024

    The motion passes without a division. Oparanya is carried in item 18 of the 19-name approval schedule. The recorded outcome is a single line: “(Question put and agreed to)”.1

What the record shows

An adverse integrity letter exists, dated and filed with the Clerk (29 July 2024), describing an open investigation and asset-preservation orders of about Sh28.9 million.3 A self-disclosed account of the same episode exists — the nominee’s own hearing testimony on 4 August 2024.3 And a committee clarification to the plenary exists, stating that the EACC and DPP had written to Parliament reporting no charges against him, and that the Committee decided on that basis.31 On this record, the House approved him — in a package of 19, by voice vote, in a single clause.

What the record does not show

No charge or case number appears in the record as of the approval date. No recorded vote exists for item 18 — or for any of the 19. A per-Member query of the vetting decision returns nothing, because the House never divided.4

Legal status: Self-disclosed by the nominee at hearing · EACC letter of 29 Jul 2024 on the parliamentary record · no case filed and no charge reported as of 7 August 2024.13
§ 3 — Appendix

Evidence notes

Three findings about the record itself — kept separate from the story, so each can be checked on its own.

1

Contrast — a decision that was divided.

On 20 June 2024, the Finance Bill 2024 (Second Reading) was decided by a recorded division: 192 to 105. Every Member’s vote was captured, name by name, and is downloadable.4 The Assembly knows how to record a vote when it chooses to — see the per-MP record.

2

Vetting approvals were never divided.

The 7 August approvals were carried by voice vote — Hansard’s “(Question put and agreed to)” is the complete record of the decision.1 Across Mzalendo’s full dataset of recorded National Assembly divisions, no division has ever been taken on a Cabinet Secretary approval.
