Act 3 · After — the accountability trail

One nominee. Three records. One glance.

On 7 August 2024 the National Assembly approved 19 Cabinet Secretary nominees. No MP's vote was recorded. This is the loop closed, shown side by side: the flag a nominee raised about himself, the transcript of what the House put to a vote, and the outcome that followed. Every line below carries its source.

The Flag

Hon. Wycliffe Ambetsa Oparanya, EGH
Nominee — Cooperatives & Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) Development
Self-disclosed ODM — party office resigned

“Nobody has come to me seeking that I record a statement over the allegations. It is purely a one-sided affair and I have never been given an opportunity to say my side of the story.”

“While there, I did not record a statement. They gave me the phones after hours.”

  • 23 Aug 2023Integrity Centre visit: Oparanya says EACC officers took his children's phones from his family; he denies he or his household were arrested.
  • 29 Jul 2024EACC writes to the Clerk: nominee's “integrity is in question”; cites a probe into alleged corruption, abuse of office and money laundering as Kakamega Governor.
  • 2 Aug 2024DPP has dropped the graft charges — and publicly sparses with EACC over it. No charges stood before the House when it voted.
The flag is his own account, given while the House was deciding his nomination — not a rumour, not an opposition claim.

The episode's documented four

Flags that reached the record — and how each ended

Public submissions (1,300+ received, 187 valid as sworn affidavits) surfaced claims on several nominees. Four of those claims are fully on the public record. Outcomes shown as the House left them.

Wycliffe Oparanya

Cooperatives & MSME Development

EACC told the Clerk his integrity was “in question”; he testified no statement was ever taken from him and his children's phones were taken at the Integrity Centre. Approved, no recorded vote.

Mining, Blue Economy & Maritime Affairs

Academic-qualification and conflict-of-interest claims surfaced in public submissions and affidavits; the committee said CUE authenticated certificates and it “exhaustively addressed” the issues. Approved, no recorded vote.
